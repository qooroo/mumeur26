At a glance

The shape of the trip

Five bases, moving west to east. One anchor activity per day, scheduled in the morning, with afternoons left free — the pace is deliberate, not a shortfall. The route also puts each city in its best September window: Paris mild throughout, Italy cooling across the month, and Greece at its annual best right at the end.

Practical notes

Staying safe

The realistic risk on this route is pickpocketing, not violence. Known hotspots: Paris Métro lines 1 and 4, and Gare du Nord; the "friendship bracelet" approach on the Montmartre steps; Rome's bus 64 and Termini; Porta Portese market; the Athens metro to Piraeus, and Monastiraki.

A cross-body bag worn in front and no phone in a back pocket handles nearly all of it.

112 is the emergency number in France, Italy and Greece alike.

Heat & pacing

Rome in mid-September still reaches about 28°C; Athens about 30°C. That is precisely why every anchor activity sits in the morning and every afternoon is free.

Ancient sites have almost no shade — the Roman Forum and the Acropolis especially. Hat, water, and shoes with grip. Acropolis marble is genuinely slippery.

Eating alone

Making lunch the main meal is the easiest solution, and it suits the pace anyway.

For dinner, booking early — 19:30 in Rome or Athens is well before the local 21:00 norm — means a calmer room and more attentive staff. Counter and bar seating is completely normal for one.

Money

Cards and Google Pay work throughout all three countries. No cash dependency.

When a card terminal offers to charge in pounds instead of euros, always decline and pay in local currency — the "convenience" rate is consistently poor.

Carry a modest amount of euro cash for Hydra, small tavernas, and the odd ferry kiosk.

Steps & stamina

Three climbs are deliberately skipped: Florence's dome, 463 one-way steps; St Peter's dome, 551; and the walk up to Piazzale Michelangelo. Lifts, buses and funiculars are used instead throughout.

Hydra is the exception — the town is built up a hillside, hence booking near the harbour.

Laundry

Three weeks needs one mid-trip wash. Rome, 16–21 September, is the natural point — it's the longest single stay in the second half.

Most hotels will do it, and self-service laundrettes are common in Trastevere and Monti.
